Russian olive Elaeagnus angustifolia

Native to western and central Asia, from the Caucasus and Iran to Mongolia and northern China.

A rare one here: only 3 on the map (#242 of 368 species).

Show Russian olive trees on the map

Russian olive is named for its likeness to the true olive, to which it is not related. Its dried fruit is one of the seven items of the Haft-sin table set out for Nowruz, the Persian spring festival. In the United States it has become invasive, spreading along rivers where cottonwoods have died.
