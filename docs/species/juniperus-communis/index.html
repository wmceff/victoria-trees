Common juniper Juniperus communis

Native to cool regions across the Northern Hemisphere, the widest range of any woody plant.

The only one on the map.

Show Common juniper tree on the map

Common juniper is taken as a medicine that must all be drunk, or it will not work (Haida). A decoction is drunk in the sweathouse and used as a purifying wash for hunters (Nlaka’pamux) or as a fumigant against death and illness (Okanagan), and juniper is one of the plants used to ritually treat berry bushes, fishing gear and people affected by nímính, the ritually powerful and vulnerable state that comes with birth, puberty, widowhood and death (Tsilhqot’in). Its Tsilhqot’in name means "raven’s arrow". Gin is named after the French and Dutch words for juniper, whose berry-like cones flavour it.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 132, 553; vol. 2, pp. 302, 341–342.
