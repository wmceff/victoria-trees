Red Alder Alnus rubra

SENĆOŦEN name: SḰOṈEȽĆ

Native to Victoria.

The City of Victoria has 64 trees of this species on record, 0.18% of the 35,125 trees on the map.

Show Red Alder trees on the map

Used by First Peoples for bowls and trays, masks, canoe bailers, paddles, firedrills. Cooking & smoking. Dye. Bark would be partially harvested for medicinal use, rectangular pieces cut from living trees.

In moist forest areas Alnus rubra will rapidly cover a former burn or clearcut, often preventing the establishment of conifers. It is a prolific seed producer, but the small, wind-dispersed seeds require an open area of mineral soil to germinate, and so skid trails and other areas disturbed by logging or fire are ideal seedbeds.
