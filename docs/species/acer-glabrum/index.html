Maple Acer glabrum

Native to western North America, from southeast Alaska to California and New Mexico.

Wildlife: Deer browse its foliage, especially in winter.

A rare one here: only 2 on the map (#263 of 368 species).

Show Maple trees on the map

Rocky Mountain maple's Ditidaht name means "drinking bowl plant". Its dense wood made snowshoe frames, bows, labrets (lip ornaments) and dishes, and its tough inner bark was woven into mats, baskets and trays (coastal and interior peoples); bark and branches were taken from living plants (Interior Plateau). In the Ts’msyen story "The Boy Taken by the Star", people burned every kind of wood to find the one that lasts longest, and it was this maple, still known as a long-lasting fuel. The Ts’msyen also traded its wood to the Haida.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 130, 341–342, 346–347, 365, 379, 400; vol. 2, pp. 122, 185.
