Callery pear Pyrus calleryana

Native to China, Korea, Taiwan and Japan.

115 on the map, the #63 most common of 368 species.

Usually in flower from mid March to early April, with white blossoms. See when Victoria's other blossom trees flower.

Show Callery pear trees on the map

Callery pear's small, hard fruits soften after frost and are then eaten by birds, which spread the seeds in their droppings; that is how it has become invasive in much of the eastern United States. It is named after Joseph-Marie Callery, a missionary in China who sent specimens to Europe. Its spring flowers have a smell often compared to rotting fish.

Cultivars in Victoria

'Chanticleer' (86 trees): A narrow, pyramidal Callery pear with white flowers in early spring and red-purple autumn colour, widely planted as a street tree.
