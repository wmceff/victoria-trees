Scouler's willow Salix scouleriana

SENĆOŦEN name: SX̱ELE¸IȽĆ

Native to western North America, from Alaska to New Mexico, including Victoria.

One of Victoria's most common trees: 455 on the map, about 1 in 77 trees (#19 of 369 species).

Show Scouler's willow trees on the map

Straits Salish peoples cut willow bark and stems from living plants for cordage and netting, leaving them to regrow; the W̱SÁNEĆ word for willow means "reefnet plant", after the willow-bark reefnets that are a Straits Salish specialty. The Secwepemc used its wood to smoke fish and build weirs, and its inner bark for lashing, sewing and cordage. Its catkins give bees pollen and nectar in early spring.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 131, 399; vol. 2, p. 191.
