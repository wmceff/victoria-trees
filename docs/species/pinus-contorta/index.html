Lodgepole pine Pinus contorta

Native to western North America, from Alaska and the Yukon to California and the Rockies, including Victoria.

63 on the map, the #79 most common of 369 species.

Show Lodgepole pine trees on the map

Shore pine, the coastal form of lodgepole pine, grows twisted and stunted on rocky shores and in bogs, which gave the species its name, contorta. Its pitch and inner bark were harvested a little at a time so the tree lived: pitch by coastal peoples such as the Haida and Gitxsan, and inner bark, for food, by interior peoples. Inland, lodgepole pine depends on fire, whose heat opens its cones to release the seeds.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 2, p. 184.
