Sitka willow Salix sitchensis

Native to western North America, from Alaska to northern California and inland to Montana, including Victoria.

A rare one here: only 6 on the map (#201 of 368 species).

Show Sitka willow trees on the map

The W̱SÁNEĆ (Saanich) word for willow means "reefnet plant": Straits Salish reefnets, said to have been given by Xals, the Creator, were made from willow bark, mostly Pacific willow's. Coastal peoples also twisted Sitka willow bark into bowstrings, and in the interior its branches and inner bark were used to build fish weirs and traps. It is named after Sitka, Alaska, where it was first collected.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 131, 368, 370, 399.
