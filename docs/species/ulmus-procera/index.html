English elm Ulmus procera

An ancient clone of the field elm, thought to come from Atina, in central Italy.

A rare one here: only 3 on the map (#242 of 368 species).

Show English elm trees on the map

English elm is not native to England: every English elm is genetically identical, and the clone is thought to be the Atinian elm that the Romans grew to support vines and probably brought to Britain. Its flowers are sterile, so it spreads only by root suckers, and its rot-resistant trunks were once hollowed out for water pipes.
