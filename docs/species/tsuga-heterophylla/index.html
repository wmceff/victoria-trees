Western hemlock Tsuga heterophylla

SENĆOŦEN name: ȾKÁ¸IȽĆ

Native to the Pacific Northwest, from southeast Alaska to northern California and inland to Idaho and Montana, including Victoria.

Western hemlock boughs are used as ritual scrubbers to protect girls at puberty and hunters (Kwakwaka’wakw, Hesquiaht, Ditidaht, Makah, Nlaka’pamux), and its branches are set in the sea to collect herring spawn. Its inner bark was eaten, and its hard knots were bent into fishhooks. The Ditidaht name means "fire goes out", because it burns poorly. Its seedlings grow on rotting stumps and logs, and wait for decades in the shade for a gap in the canopy.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 340, 344, 351; vol. 2, pp. 179, 185, 195, 303.
