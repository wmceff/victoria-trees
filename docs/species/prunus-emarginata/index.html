Bitter cherry Prunus emarginata

SENĆOŦEN name: SȻEȾEṈIȽĆ

Native to western North America, from British Columbia to California and east to the Rockies, including Victoria.

Wildlife: Cedar waxwings eat its cherries and spread the seeds.

191 on the map, the #37 most common of 368 species.

Usually in flower from mid April to mid May, with white blossoms. See when Victoria's other blossom trees flower.

Show Bitter cherry trees on the map

Bitter cherry bark is peeled in strips from living trees to decorate baskets, wrap tool joints and make cordage (Coast Salish and interior peoples), and it is one of the ten barks in a W̱SÁNEĆ (Saanich) family medicine for tuberculosis. Its bark is also used in poultices and infusions (Kwakwaka’wakw). As its name says, its cherries are too bitter for people to eat.

Indigenous uses from Nancy Turner, Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge (2014), vol. 1, pp. 343, 462; vol. 2, p. 189.
