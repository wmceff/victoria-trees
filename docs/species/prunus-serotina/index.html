Black cherry Prunus serotina

Native to North and Central America, from southern Canada to Guatemala.

The only one on the map.

Show Black cherry tree on the map

Black cherry is perhaps the premier cabinet timber of the United States, sold simply as "cherry" and prized for its strong orange tones. A scratched young twig smells of almonds, a sign of the cyanide compounds that make its wilted leaves poisonous to livestock. Its juice can be used to mix drinks, hence its other name, rum cherry.
