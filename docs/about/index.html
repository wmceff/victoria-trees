About Victoria Trees

Victoria Trees is a map of the 35,125 trees on public land in Victoria, BC, from 376 species. Find the trees near you, tap one to learn its species, where it comes from and its SENĆOŦEN name, or browse by species, neighbourhood and blossom season.

Open the map

Where the data comes from

The trees are from the City of Victoria's trees dataset, last refreshed on November 21, 2026. We have amended and added to the data where necessary, including trees from other surveys and nearby municipalities.

If a tree is missing, it's most likely on private property, which the City doesn't keep track of. Apps like iNaturalist or PictureThis can help identify it. Spotted a wrong or missing tree, or have an idea? Send feedback.

SENĆOŦEN names

Victoria stands on the traditional territories of the lək̓ʷəŋən peoples, the Songhees and Esquimalt Nations. Where known, SENĆOŦEN tree names are provided, with a link to a website where you can hear the pronunciation. SENĆOŦEN is the language of the W̱SÁNEĆ People, and a member of the North Straits Salish dialect continuum along with lək̓ʷəŋən.

Sources and credits

Descriptions are sourced from Wikipedia, and Indigenous uses for trees native to this area are primarily sourced from Nancy Turner's Ancient Pathways, Ancestral Knowledge.

Download the data

Everything on the map is in two files, rebuilt from the City's data with the corrections and additions above:

  • trees.csv: one row per tree, with its location, species, common and botanical names, height, trunk diameter, street or park, and neighbourhood.
  • trees.json: the compact file the map loads, with each species' details (native range, SENĆOŦEN name, description).

There's also a plain-text summary of the site for AI assistants at llms.txt.

Linking to the map

The map takes two parameters, so you can link straight to a species or an area:

  • ?species= highlights a species by its page's name, eg. ?species=prunus-x-yedoensis for the Yoshino cherries. Separate several with commas.
  • ?bounds= frames an area as west,south,east,north in degrees, eg. ?species=quercus-garryana&bounds=-123.38,48.405,-123.36,48.42 for the Garry oaks around Beacon Hill Park.

Using it on your phone

Add the map to your home screen for quick access. On iPhone, use the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
